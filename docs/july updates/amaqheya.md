The sources and our previous discussion provide details on two different individuals named **Qeea** (or **Qea**) and offer linguistic context that supports your suspicion regarding the Xhosa variants of the name.

### What Happened to Qeea?

The historical records describe two distinct women named Qeea, both of San origin, who were captured during conflicts with Sotho leaders:

1.  **Qeea, Wife of Moshoeshoe I (c. 1824):** Following a conflict at Qeme mountain where a San group under **Chief Quu** fled, two young San girls were left behind. One of these girls, **Qeea**, was taken to Thaba Bosiu, where she became one of **King Moshoeshoe’s wives**. She gave birth to a son who was noted for having San features but was described as "very good looking". A parallel tradition suggests she may have been the daughter of a San hunter who allowed her to marry Moshoeshoe in exchange for hunting rights in the Maluti mountains.
2.  **Qeea, Captive of Chief Jonathan (c. 1871–1872):** A different girl named **Qeea** was captured during the raid led by the Sotho chief **Jonathan** against **Chief Soai’s** band. Jonathan, who was a "bitter enemy of the San," treated her as property. He gave her to one of his **vassals as a wife**, but with the explicit and harsh condition that **all children born of the marriage would belong to Jonathan**.

### Could "Qeea" be "Qhiya" or "Qheya" in Xhosa?

There is strong linguistic and historical evidence in the sources to support the idea that "Qeea" is a phonetic rendering of a name that would appear as **Qheya** or **Qhiya** in Xhosa.

- **The Term "Qheya":** Historically, **iQheya** (or _uQheya_) was a term used by Xhosa speakers to refer to people of Khoe or San descent. This aligns with the "monumental absorption" of San and Khoe terms into Xhosa described in the sources [Conversation History]. The name "Qeea" recorded by Sotho and colonial observers is likely their transcription of this click-based name.
- **Phonetic Evolution:** As we discussed regarding the word _axab_ potentially becoming _qwab_, the post-alveolar click represented by **"Q"** was the standard way Nguni speakers adopted Khoe-San sounds [Conversation History, 235]. The variant **Qea** or **Qeea** matches the structure of Khoe-San roots that were integrated into Xhosa speech.
- **Context of Hybridization:** The sources note that the people in the Quthing and Komani regions spoke **Seputhi**, a hybrid dialect of Xhosa and Sesotho. This dialect frequently used Xhosa-influenced versions of San words—for example, the San man **Qing** used the term **Qweqwete** for initiates, which researchers identify as a version of the Xhosa word **abakwetha**.
- **The Name "Qhiya":** While **Qhiya** is a modern Xhosa word for a head-wrap or headscarf (a definition not explicitly in the sources but common in Xhosa), the phonetic similarity between **Qeea**, **Qea**, and **Qhiya/Qheya** points to a shared linguistic origin rooted in the integration of San individuals into Nguni-speaking households.

In summary, the name **Qeea** represents a lineage of San women absorbed into Sotho and Nguni royal or vassal families, and its phonetic structure is almost certainly a variation of the Xhosa **Qheya** or **Qhiya**.
