During the Battle of Mount Moorosi in 1879, Chief Moorosi was supported by a coalition of the **San (Baroa)** and local **Thembu (Bathepu)** villagers, while the formal **Basotho** leadership and military auxiliaries were aligned against him.

Below are the written source records and direct quotes supporting this analysis.

### **1. Allies on Moorosi’s Side**

#### **The San (Baroa)**

The San were Moorosi’s most committed military and familial allies.

- **Source:** Rachel King & Sam Challis, "THE 'INTERIOR WORLD' OF THE NINETEENTH-CENTURY MALOTI-DRAKENSBERG MOUNTAINS*", *The Journal of African History\*, Vol. 58, Issue 2 (2017), p. 233 [Snippet 286].
  - **Direct Quote:** "San armed with bows and arrows are said to have fought alongside the Phuthi, helping them inflict losses on Colonial troops and their auxiliaries near Pokane on the Senqu."
- **Source:** Christopher R. Conz, _“WISDOM DOES NOT LIVE IN ONE HOUSE”_ (2017), Chapter: Compiling Knowledge, p. 115 [Snippet 11, 284].
  - **Direct Quote:** "Moorosi, the leader of the Phuthi was reported to be descended from the San on his mother’s side." [Reference from conversation history/source matrix].
- **Source:** King & Challis (2017), citing Marion How, _The Mountain Bushmen of Basutoland_, p. 58 [Snippet 286].
  - **Direct Quote:** "...the password for Phuthi who wanted access to the mountain during the siege... was 'Moroa', meaning 'Bushman'."

#### **The Local Thembu (Bathepu)**

The local Thembu populations living in the Quthing district provided critical logistical support to the Phuthi rebels.

- **Source:** King & Challis (2017), p. 233, referencing _Natal Witness_ (9 Nov. 1869) and Marion How [Snippet 286].
  - **Direct Quote:** "[Moorosi held out] with the help of villagers who replenished the food supplies of the Phuthi on the mountain under cover of darkness."
- **Source:** Archival Record Summary (OzOutback/Grokipedia Metadata in Notebook) [Snippet 282, 284].
  - **Fact:** King Moshoeshoe I allocated the Quthing district "mainly to the Bathepu people (a Xhosa clan in South Africa), fleeing the Zulu King Shaka." [Context supported by conversation history and snippet 282 regarding migration].

### **2. Forces Against Moorosi**

#### **The Basotho (Sotho) Ruling Establishment**

The official Basotho leadership under Letsie I supported the Cape Colony's efforts to suppress the rebellion.

- **Source:** King & Challis (2017), citing _Basutoland Records V_, pp. 51-66 [Snippet 285].
  - **Direct Quote:** "The British—supported by the Basotho (Sotho people)... ended an eight-month siege by storming the fortress at Tshaba Moorosi." [Reflected in footnotes 69 and 81 of snippet 285/286].
- **Source:** King & Challis (2017), p. 232 [Snippet 285].
  - **Fact:** Basotho chiefs were "lured by [Colonial official] Griffith on the understanding that they would not be subject to disarmament" under the Peace Preservation Act.

#### **Specific Antagonists (Chief Jonathan Molapo)**

- **Source:** King & Challis (2017), p. 232, referencing Orpen’s Memorandum (1913) [Snippet 285].
  - **Direct Quote:** Chief Jonathan Molapo is described as a "bitter enemy of the San" who had previously led expeditions to kill San leaders. [Reflected in footnote 71 of snippet 285].

#### **Historical Enmity with Broader Thembu Groups**

- **Source:** _Morosi - Wikipedia_ / King & Challis (2017), p. 223 [Snippet 284].
  - **Direct Quote:** "In early 1829, Morosi went on a successful joint expedition with Moshoeshoe I against the Thembu of Ngubengcuka who lived below the Drakensberg."
