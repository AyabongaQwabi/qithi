# The History of the abaThembu

## Origins: The Mbo Nation

The Thembu are not Xhosa. This is why the Eastern Cape has a Xhosa King, a Thembu King, and a Mpondo King — the language is one, isiXhosa, but they are not one nation. Unlike the Zulu Kingdom, which absorbed many distinct nations into one through war, the Thembu kept their identity intact.

The Thembu originate from the eMbo Empire, traced in oral tradition to the great lakes of northern Africa. What distinguishes the Thembu from the Xhosa, Ndebele, and Zulu is that those nations descend from the common ancestor Nguni, while the Thembu are Mbo people. The Mpondo, Xesibe, and Bomvana are also Mbo people, distinguished from the Thembu by different father ancestors.

Zwide was the first man to leave eMbo, and he is the progenitor of the Thembu line. This Zwide is not to be confused with Zwide of the Ndwandwe — this is the Zwide of the great lakes.

Zwide had a younger brother, Sibiside. Sibiside begat Njanya the eldest, Dlamini, and Mkhize of KwaZulu-Natal. Dlamini is said to be the progenitor of the Swazi people of eSwatini. Mkhize is Mbo, not Zulu, though his people were assimilated into the Zulu nation during Shaka's time. Njanya crossed the Mzimvubu River and begat Mpondo, Mpondomise, Xesibe, and Nomafu, the mother of the amaBomvana nation.

## The Migration South

Zwide's line travelled down from the great lakes, passing Ethiopia. Zwide begat Malandela — not Malandela of the Zulu — who continued down Africa past Malawi. Malandela's grave is believed to lie near the Mozambique–South Africa border, at a place called Farazela. All the nations, on arriving in South Africa, arrived first in KwaZulu-Natal.

Malandela begat Mbulali. Mbulali begat Njanye — not to be confused with Njanya, Sibiside's son. Njanye begat Thembu.

## Thembu and His Sons

Thembu settled with his people at a place known as Mbabane, then moved to KwaMsinga in Natal. There he begat two sons, Mvelase and Qhudeni. The name Qhudeni is said to come from a rooster that would crow when Shaka's armies drew near, so that the nation could neither prepare for war nor escape. Mvelase founded the tribe known as abaThembu bakwaMvelase, who remain in Natal to this day.

Thembu also begat a younger son, Ndilo, with whom he travelled across the uMzimvubu River. Thembu passed away at Dedesi. Ndilo crossed the Mzimvubu and settled at a place called Msana, believed to be in or near KwaZulu-Natal, where he eventually died.

## Ndilo's Sons and the Commoner Houses

Ndilo's sons included Ntongakazi (also known as Dumakazi), Bhomoyi, Mncotshe, Mqoma, Mdlane, and Jali.

The houses of Mncotshe, Mqoma, Mdlane, and Jali are not widely known as Thembu nations because their mothers were commoners. Black cows were slaughtered for them so that the Thembu could accept them and grow in numbers. The respected houses were those of Bhomoyi, whose mother came from royalty, and Ntongakazi.

## Bhomoyi at Qacha's Nek: "Vela Bambhentsele"

Bhomoyi left Msana for Qacha's Nek in Lesotho, where he fought the Basotho for land and took it. When the Basotho came to ask him to leave so they could farm their land, he replied: _"Andizuhamba de ndizobe ndibentselwa zintombi zabeSotho"_ — "I will not leave until the Basotho maidens are presented to me naked," from _ukubhentsa_, to go naked. The Basotho king sent fifteen young women.

This is the origin of the Thembu praise **"Vela Bambhentsele."** There is a common confusion attributing this praise to Dlomo after his war with Hlanga, but the originator of the praise is Bhomoyi.

## Ceduma and Mnguti

Bhomoyi begat Ceduma. Ceduma begat Mnguti (rendered in some accounts as Mgude).

Mnguti begat two sons: Mngxongo, whose mother came from the Mpondo royal family, and Ntoyi of the right-hand house, whose mother was a commoner.

## The Coming of the amaQwathi

Shortly after Mnguti's sons were born, a section of the amaXesibe arrived, led by Mtshutshumbe — son of Ntozabantu, son of Hlabe, son of Bhimpi, son of Thobeja. They were travelling to the amaGcaleka, but because the Thembu and the Xesibe had a history of war, Mtshutshumbe hid his Xesibe identity by renaming his people the **amaQwathi**, after a cow he had been given as a gift at his graduation from initiation school.

Mtshutshumbe presented a tribute of cattle to Mnguti, who took his people into the Thembu nation and settled them at Sigubudwini, near eNgcobo. Guided by his father, Mngxongo carried out the allocation of their land. In return, the amaQwathi gave Mngxongo cattle that were all dark brown — _ntsundu_ — and this is preserved to this day in the praise of Mngxongo as **Ntsundu**.

Mngxongo did not return from this mission. His father Mnguti, searching for him, became lost and drowned with his dogs in the Qethume River.

## The Overlooked Heir

Mngxongo's heir was Mphosesebeni, who was refused his place on the throne by the royal family. To this day, when the names of Mnguti and Mngxongo are mentioned, it becomes awkward ebaThenjini, because the rightful king was overlooked. It was Mphosesebeni's uncle, Ntoyi, who took the royal throne.

## Ntoyi, Ntande, and Zima the Regent

Ntoyi begat Ntande and Zima. Ntande's mother was the first wife and Zima's mother the second, but Zima was born before Ntande. Ntande remained the rightful heir. When Ntoyi died, the chieftaincy was handed to Zima for safekeeping while Ntande was still young.

During this period the amaTola arrived, led by Mkhume and his brother. It was the amaTola who introduced the use of spears to the abaThembu.

When Ntande came of age, Zima refused to vacate the seat. With the help of the amaTola, Ntande resorted to a trick: as the tale goes, he faked his own death to lure Zima and his men into a kraal, where they were ambushed. Only then did Ntande take the reins.

Zima was believed to own a sacred cow called Mayemane, which could give milk at any time of year without a calf — a cow believed in those days to have magical powers, and spoken of as the ancestor of the modern milk cow.

## Ntande's Children: Nxeko, Maya, and the Twins Qithi and Cube

Ntande begat Nxeko, Maya, and the twins **Qithi** and **Cube**.

Qithi and Cube's mothers were commoners, and black cows were slaughtered for them as well — the same ritual of incorporation performed generations earlier for the commoner-house sons of Ndilo.

## Nxeko, First Legitimate King

During the sixteenth century, Nxeko led the migration from Dedesi to Msana in the Mthatha district. Nxeko is regarded as the first legitimate king of the abaThembu, because it was he who assimilated fragments of various peoples — amaBomvana, amaVundle, amaMpondomise, and amaMfengu — to build his kingdom.

Nxeko begat Hlanga, by a Mpondo woman of the Mqiha clan; Dlomo, the eldest, from another wife; and the twins Balisa (also called Bhejula) and Ndungwana, from a third wife.

The wives had not been classified into ranks from major to minor, but it was clear that Hlanga, whose mother was a princess, was the most likely royal successor.

## Ndungwana and the amaMfene

At that time the amaMfene came asking to be incorporated into the Thembu nation. They were duly received, and Ndungwana was instructed to allocate sites to them. Ndungwana never returned from his mission — he remained with the strangers, enjoying all the respect accorded to him, and installed himself as their nkosi.

Whenever Dlomo slaughtered a cow, he would send _inxaxheba_ — the right hindquarter — to Ndungwana, his junior; and Hlanga would likewise send his _nxaxheba_ to his own junior, Balisa.

One day cows were slaughtered in the homes of both Dlomo and Hlanga. Dlomo shared meat with Welangaye, a maiden of the Mpemvu clan, and she burst out in praise of how Dlomo had effectively installed himself as king, shedding tears of joy.

## Dlomo Takes the Throne

It is widely known that Dlomo usurped the throne and took the kingdom from Hlanga.

## The Royal Line After Dlomo

Dlomo begat Hala and Mawisa.

From Mawisa came the line of the amaTshatshu: Mawisa begat Thukwa, Thukwa begat Xhoba, and Xhoba begat Tshatshu.

From Hala came the royal line: Hala begat Madiba, Madiba begat Thato, Thato begat Zondwa. From Zondwa onwards the line is confirmed by modern sources:

Zondwa → Ndaba → Ngubengcuka → Mthikrakra → Ngangelizwe → Dalindyebo → Jongilizwe → Sabata → Buyelekhaya
