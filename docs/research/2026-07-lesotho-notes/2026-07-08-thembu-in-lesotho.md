The history of the **Thembu (abaThembu or Amatemba)** in Lesotho is defined by ancient migrations, extensive intermarriage with the San, and complex political alliances with the Basotho and BaPhuthi nations.

### **Origins and Migration into Lesotho**

While the parent Thembu tribes are primarily associated with the Eastern Cape and Tembuland, their presence in Lesotho dates back several centuries:

- **Early Settlement:** Historical accounts note that a few "brave Nguni," including Thembu groups, crossed the Drakensberg to settle in the hilly areas of what is now Lesotho.
- **The First Emigrants:** Oral traditions identify **Mnguti** as the first recorded Thembu to leave Lesotho around the 1500s [Conversation History]. He migrated toward Herschel and later moved to **eNgcobo**, leaving behind his sons Mngxongo, Nxego, and Sebeni [Conversation History].
- **Lineage Splits:** According to historical records, Mnguti had only one brother, **Maya** [Conversation History]. The **ama-Maya** were a specific Thembu tribe reigned over by Mnyibeshe; they were eventually driven out of their territory and sought refuge with Palo.

### **San-Thembu Hybridization: The AmaQithi**

A central theme of Thembu history in Lesotho is their profound physical and cultural integration with the San (Baroa).

- **The "Tambu'ki Bushmen":** In the late 18th century, travelers reported an amalgamated group known as the **"Tambu'ki Bushmen"** (or "Chinese Bushmen") living near the Tsomo River. These were San who had intermarried with pioneering Thembu clans, resulting in a people with lighter complexions.
- **The AmaQithi Clan:** This clan represents a direct **San-Thembu heritage** that resided in the **Quthing** region during the era of Chief Moorosi [Conversation History]. The name of the district, Quthing, is the Sotho pronunciation of the Xhosa term **emaQithini**, the ancestral home of this clan [Conversation History].
- **Cultural Markers (Ingqithi):** The Thembu and AmaQithi adopted the San custom of **Ingqithi**—the ritual amputation of a terminal finger joint—as a sign of their San heritage [208, 224, Conversation History].

### **The Sayings of Bhomoyi**

Historical evidence confirms a Thembu presence in Lesotho during the time of **Bhomoyi**. He is famously associated with the saying "**Vela bambhentsele**," a nickname earned after defeating Sotho forces [Conversation History]. When the defeated Sotho requested land, Bhomoyi reportedly responded by demanding that Sotho women lie naked beside him [Conversation History].

### **Political Alliances and Conflict**

The Thembu in Lesotho were frequently involved in the power struggles of the 19th century:

- **Joint Expeditions:** In early 1829, **King Moshoeshoe I** and the Phuthi chief **Moorosi** conducted joint military expeditions against the Thembu under **Chief Ngubengcuka** below the Drakensberg to capture cattle.
- **The 1879 War:** Following the fall of **Mount Moorosi** in 1879, many San-Thembu families, such as the line of **Qwabi Joka**, migrated from the Quthing region to the **Queenstown (Komani)** and **Lady Frere** areas of the Eastern Cape [Conversation History]. Qwabi Joka famously named his eldest son **Bushman** to honor his San ancestry [Conversation History].

### **Thembu Leaders in the Colonial Era**

In later years, Thembu leaders in the region were often designated as "loyal" or "anti-colonial" by the British:

- **Nonesi:** She was the daughter of the Mpondo chief Faku and the wife of the Thembu paramount **Ngubencuka**.
- **Tambookie Location:** In 1853, following the eighth frontier war, a large tract of land north of Queenstown (the Tambookie Location) was allocated to approximately 40,000 "loyal" abaThembu, including the **AmaGcina** under Tyopo and the **amaHala** under Ndarala.
