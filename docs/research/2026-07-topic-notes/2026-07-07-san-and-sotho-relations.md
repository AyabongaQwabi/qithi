The historical relationship between the Xhosa, Sotho, Phuti, and San groups is defined by a complex spectrum of **physical and cultural hybridization**, ranging from deep familial alliances to intense military conflict.

### **Relation between Xhosa and Sotho -> Phuti**

- The **BaPhuti** chiefdom was formed through the **intermarriage of the amaZizi** (an Nguni group related to the Xhosa) and the **Sotho** of the Eastern Free State.
- Because of this mixed heritage, the Phuti speak **Seputhi**, which is described as a **"hybrid dialect"** between Xhosa and Sesotho.
- Under the leadership of **Chief Moorosi**, the Phuti nation functioned as a federation that incorporated various refugee groups, including those of Xhosa, Sotho, and San origin.
- The Phuti culture represents a "monumental absorption" of different traditions, blending Nguni and Sotho social structures.

### **Relation between San and Phuti**

- The relationship between the San and the Phuti was characterized by a **prolonged and symbiotic alliance** that was both political and familial.
- **Intermarriage** was so extensive that Phuti royalty often had San blood; **Chief Moorosi** himself was reported to be of San descent on his mother's side, and his father, Mokuoane, married the sister of the **San chief Quu**.
- During the **Difaqane**, the San sheltered and supported the Phuti in mountain caves, providing them with game and wild foods when they had no cattle.
- San warriors were essential military allies who fought alongside the Phuti during the **1879 siege of Mount Moorosi**.
- The depth of this bond is illustrated by the Phuti using the word **"Moroa" (meaning "Bushman")** as their high-security password for the mountain fortress.
- Phuti leaders like **Mapote** (Moorosi's son) lived and **painted rock art** with the San, reflecting a shared intellectual and artistic culture.

### **Relation between San and Sotho**

- Sotho traditions initially acknowledged the San as the **"great people in their origin"** and referred to them as "princes" who were the original "lords of the soil".
- Early Sotho groups, such as the **Phetla and Taung**, established close ties with the San, often marrying San women and learning survival techniques like **digging game pits** from them.
- **King Moshoeshoe I** actively sought to befriend San communities and had at least **two San wives**, one of whom was named **Qeea**.
- Despite these ties, the relationship soured over **cattle and horse raiding**; San bands led by **Chief Soai** frequently plundered the livestock of Sotho chiefs like Molapo.
- This led to a shift from brotherhood to **bitter enmity**, culminating in Sotho chiefs like **Jonathan and Joel** organizing expeditions to "exterminate" San bands, eventually leading to the death of Soai in 1872.
- Sotho ritual life remains profoundly influenced by the San; for instance, some traditions claim the San **taught the Sotho their form of circumcision** and initiation rites.
- Many San individuals were eventually **absorbed into Sotho society**, adopting the Sesotho language while their descendants continued to be identified as **Baroa** (people of San descent).
