**Khoboko** (sometimes referred to as Khoboka) was a prominent San leader in the Maloti-Drakensberg region whose lineage played a crucial role in formalizing the long-standing political and familial alliance between the San and the **BaPhuthi** people.

### **Lineage and Leadership**

Khoboko was the father of **Chief Soai**, who is historically recognized as the last independent San chief in Lesotho [Conversation History, 520]. His people were based in the rugged highlands of the Maloti mountains, specifically at **Sehonghong Cave** (also known as Mangolong), which served as a major stronghold and a center for San artistic expression [Conversation History, 521].

### **The San-Phuti Alliance**

Khoboko’s people are best known for their deep integration with the BaPhuthi chiefdom under **Chief Moorosi**. This relationship went beyond mere proximity:

- **Intermarriage:** Historical records state that the **sons of Khoboko intermarried extensively with the Phuti**, effectively merging the two groups' royal and social lineages [Conversation History, 520].
- **Symbiotic Partnership:** Under the leadership of Khoboko and later his son Soai, the San provided military support and survival expertise to the Phuti. During the disruptions of the **Difaqane**, the San sheltered Phuti refugees in their caves and provided them with game when they had no livestock [Conversation History, 396, 460].
- **Cultural Exchange:** This bond was so strong that Moorosi frequently visited Sehonghong Cave to watch Khoboko’s people paint rock art, while San warriors eventually fought alongside the Phuti during colonial sieges [Conversation History, 521, 588].

### **Conflict and Dispersal**

Despite their strong alliances with the Phuti, Khoboko's people faced "bitter enmity" from Sotho leaders such as **Molapo** and his son **Jonathan** [Conversation History]. This friction was primarily caused by the San conducting persistent **cattle and horse raids** on Sotho livestock to sustain their mountain-based lifestyle [Conversation History, 469].

In the early 1870s, Sotho expeditions led by Jonathan and Joel targeted Khoboko’s band. These expeditions resulted in the death of **Chief Soai** around 1872, after he was tracked to a pool near Sehonghong Cave [Conversation History, 484]. Following this defeat, the remnants of Khoboko’s people were largely scattered:

- Many survivors were absorbed into the Phuti or Sotho populations as **Baroa** (people of San descent) [Conversation History, 548, 549].
- **Qing**, who is believed to be the son of Soai and grandson of Khoboko, escaped the destruction and later became a famous guide and the primary source of San mythology for colonial researchers [Conversation History, 272].
