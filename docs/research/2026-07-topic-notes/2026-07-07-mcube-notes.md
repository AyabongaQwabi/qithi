### **Thembu (Amatemba) in Lesotho**

The presence of the Thembu in and around Lesotho is defined by historical migrations, intermarriage with the San, and military conflicts:

- **Migration and Settlement:** While the parent Thembu tribes are primarily associated with the Transkei and Eastern Cape, historical accounts note that a few "brave Nguni" (including Thembu groups) crossed the Drakensberg to settle in the hilly areas of what is now Lesotho. Early Sotho groups like the **Maphetla** are also reported to have traveled into Tembuland during their own migrations before returning to Basutoland.
- **San-Thembu Hybridity:** There is a significant history of **intermarriage between the San and the Thembu**. This resulted in many Thembu individuals having a lighter complexion, which was noted by early travelers like Le Vaillant. One San group, known as the **"Tambu'ki Bushmen,"** was reported to be an amalgamated group of San and Thembu living near the Tsomo River in the late 18th century.
- **Conflict and Alliances:** In 1829, **King Moshoeshoe I** and the Phuthi chief **Moorosi** conducted joint military expeditions against the Thembu under **Chief Ngubengcuka** below the Drakensberg to capture cattle. Conversely, some Thembu chiefs, such as **Jumba**, were known to be on friendly terms with San families in the mid-19th century.
- **The AmaQithi Link:** As noted in our previous discussion, the **AmaQithi clan** is a group of **San-Thembu heritage** that migrated from the Quthing region of Lesotho to the Queenstown/Lady Frere area following the fall of Mount Moorosi in 1879 [Conversation History].

### **Specific Names and Lineages**

- **Mqithi:** This name is fundamentally linked to the geography of Lesotho and the heritage of San-descended groups. The district of **Quthing** is the Sotho pronunciation of the Xhosa term **emaQithini**, a name shared by the **AmaQithi** clan of San-Thembu heritage [Conversation History].
- **Maya:** The sources mention the **ama-Maya**, which was a specific **Thembu tribe** reigned over by Mnyibeshe; they were driven out of their territory during the time of the chief Ndaba and sought refuge with Palo. Additionally, the **Bamaiyane** (people of Maiyane) are a Sotho clan and an offshoot of the **Bakuena of Monaheng**. **Mayara** (also known as Ntalime) was a principal son of King Moshoeshoe I.
- **Mcube:** While the name "Mcube" is not explicitly listed as an individual in these sources, there is a reference to a place named **e-Macubeni** (or Machubeni) located at the head of the Imvane River.
- **Ntande, Bhomoyi, and Mnguti:** These specific names **do not appear** in the provided historical texts or genealogies.

In summary, the **Thembu** presence in Lesotho is deeply intertwined with the history of the **San** and the **BaPhuthi**, particularly through the **AmaQithi** lineage and the shared history of the **Quthing** region.
